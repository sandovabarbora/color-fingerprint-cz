A field study, 2026

Color Fingerprint of Czech Advertising

Ten publicly available spots, sampled at one frame per second, clustered in CIE‑LAB color space; the picture that emerges, and the moves it suggests.

By Barbora Šandová. Source on GitHub.

Method

Frames sampled at one per second from public uploads. Per‑frame palettes via K‑means (k = 5) in canonical CIE‑LAB, where Euclidean distance approximates perceptual color difference. Brand metrics aggregate across all retained frames of each spot.

Each spot becomes a contact sheet: every frame a vertical column, every column split top to bottom by the K‑means cluster sizes. Dominant colors fill more space; accents stay narrow.

02

Diversity meets saturation tension

Two independent axes. Quadrants split at the corpus median. Dot color reads each brand's anchor swatch.

AAA Auto's yellow occupies 0.001% of screen-time.

Their brand color is a saturated logo yellow; their picture is a dim gray catalog of cars and dealerships. The logo carries the brand. The picture does not.

03

Brand color, on-screen color

Distance, in degrees on the hue wheel, between each brand's anchor swatch and the dominant chromatic color the spot actually puts on screen. Bars fill the swatch; reference at 90° marks the threshold beyond which the two are, perceptually, different colors.

04

Patterns

01

Brand color and on-screen color are usually different colors.

Across the corpus, the median gap between the brand's anchor hue and the dominant on-screen hue sits near 125°. Only two spots compress that gap below 20°: McDonald's Sýrová sezóna (8°, the cheese is brand yellow) and Kofola MéněVíce (11°, the world is cola brown). Mattoni (167°), AAA Auto (159°) and T-Mobile Vánoce (153°) sit at the far end. Their pictures live somewhere a hue-wheel away from their logos.

Why it mattersBrand color is a logo property, on-screen color is a world property. Most brands accept the gap. Closing it (Sýrová sezóna's product flood, Kofola's monothematic brown) is a deliberate creative move, not a default, and only really works when the product itself is the brand color.

02

Saturation tension is a category trait, not a creative choice.

Banking (Fio std 0.05) and automotive (AAA std 0.04) flatten the saturation arc; the spot lives at one register from start to finish. Performative formats swing: Mc'n'Roll (0.20), Mattoni (0.19), T-Mobile Vánoce (0.17) build by alternating dim and bright. Two clusters, three- to five-fold separation, almost no overlap.

Why it mattersIf your category sits at the low end, raising saturation tension is a departure from genre, worth doing when the brief calls for emotional storytelling, expensive when it doesn't. Decide which side of the gap you want to live on, then stay there.

03

The same brand can play color in opposite ways across spots.

McDonald's Sýrová sezóna runs brand yellow on 48% of screen-time; Mc'n'Roll on 25%. Same brand, same yellow, half the presence. The first is a product flood. The second cedes the picture to Přemek Forejt's musical world and lets the logo carry the brand.

Why it mattersBrand color is not a constant. It is a creative parameter. Decide what job you want it to do in each spot: heroic background, or accent inside someone else's story. The two are different jobs, even when the brief says "stay on brand."

04

Holiday creative converges across categories.

Penny, Vodafone, Pilsner, Kofola and T-Mobile, five categories with five Vánoční spots, sit within a tight band of palette diversity (14 to 24) and saturation tension (0.09 to 0.17), reaching for warm reds and dim browns regardless of sector. Vánoce flattens sectoral signatures.

Why it mattersA Christmas spot that looks distinctively yours requires active resistance to the season's shared palette. The default move, warm-red-and-candlelight, produces a spot that could belong to a supermarket, a telco or a brewery interchangeably.

05

Playbook

Five moves a strategist or creative director can run on next week's pitch.

01

Set numerical color targets before the creative review, not after.

When scoping a new spot, agree with the client on target ranges for each axis. Say diversity around 22, saturation tension around 0.10, brand-color presence around 40%, arc shape: peak. The corpus here is a starter benchmark. This catches "make it more on-brand" arguments in the brief stage, before they cost a re-shoot in post.

02

Run the same analysis on the client's competitive set during pitch.

Show them where their category currently sits on the diversity by tension map. Then say where their spot will land, and where the next three competitors land. Distinctiveness becomes a coordinate, not a vibe. Especially useful with categories where every brand looks the same (banking, telco, automotive).

03

Use the brand-color gap as a diagnostic, not a goal.

If a brand's on-screen color is 120° away from its logo, that is usually fine. Picture and logo do different jobs. Closing the gap (Sýrová sezóna's 8°, Kofola's 11°) is a deliberate creative move that only makes sense when the product itself is the brand color. Stop framing brand presence as "more is better"; frame it as "what's the right amount for THIS spot's job."

04

Treat the Vánoce cluster as a warning, not a destination.

Five Vánoční spots from five sectors land in the same patch of the map. If you're pitching seasonal creative, decide explicitly: do you want to be in the comfort zone or visibly outside it. Either is fine; landing inside accidentally is not. Same logic applies to any genre cluster (sport, premium-image, family).

05

Score dailies, not just the finished cut.

The same pipeline runs on rough footage in minutes. If the agreed brief said "high saturation tension, low brand presence" and the dailies are scoring low / high, you've caught the drift before it is baked into the edit. Cheap insurance on expensive shoots.
